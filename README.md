# Amazon Prime Music — UX case study

UX case study by **Suvarna Myneni**, Senior Product / UX Designer. I designed the Amazon Music Prime experience on Android inside the Amazon Digital Music Store, mobile-first, with browsing and discovery as the focus.

**Live site:** https://suvarnamyneni.github.io/amazon-prime-music-case-study/

**Interactive prototype:** https://suvarnamyneni.github.io/amazon-prime-music-case-study/prototype/ (a clickable recreation of the key screens and flows, with placeholder example content. Design prototype by Suvarna Myneni, for portfolio purposes, not an Amazon product.)

## Summary

Prime Music gave Prime members a curated catalogue of songs, playlists, and back-catalogue titles to stream ad-free and download, included with Prime. To launch fast, it was built inside the existing Digital Music Store and Music Library (“acquire, play, manage”). That decision shaped the experience: customers were confused about ownership, could not find Prime Music in a store, and saw buy buttons everywhere. Research, prototyping, and last-minute pivots got the product to launch on Android. A post-launch diary study and journey map led to a Prime-only area that was fully separate from the store.

## Page sections

The page uses the shared case-study template and `case-study.css`.

1. **My Role:** UX Designer for the Digital Music Store on Android.
2. **Design Process:** discover, define, ideate, prototype, and test and deliver.
3. **Problem:** streaming as the default, the good/fast/cheap architecture decision, and the waterfall process.
4. **Stakeholders:** PMs, the lead and platform designers, engineering, QA, and leadership.
5. **End Users:** Prime members, non-Prime music buyers, existing owners, and lean-back vs. lean-forward listeners.
6. **Research Methods:** a card sort (8 participants), a large-scale playlist survey, a button test (16 participants), an extended beta and guerrilla testing, a post-launch diary study, and customer and market research (customers listen to 19% of their library; collectors stream 4× as much).
7. **Competitive Analysis:** the streaming landscape and the team’s positioning. The source has no feature-by-feature scorecard; the table only restates that.
8. **User Personas:** representative composites of the segments and goals in the source, in one sideways-scrolling row. There are no formal research personas.
9. **Design Decisions:** design principles; playlists plus genres; the bargain bin; a single mood and genre filter list; the store SWOT; the $0.00 vs. +Add button; and the navigation and playback pivots.
10. **Prototype:** sketches, low-fi wireframes, high-fidelity comps, sliced prototypes, and the CX spec journeys, plus a link to the [interactive prototype](prototype/).
11. **Final Design:** Android screens.
12. **Outcome:** the Android launch, the post-launch redesign, customer quotes, and what I learned.
13. **Metrics:** +71% median active customers, +50% active days, +36% listening hours, and +31% new-customer retention. The source labels these as measured results after the Android redesign. It gives no period, baseline, or method.
