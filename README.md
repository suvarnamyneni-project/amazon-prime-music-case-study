# Amazon Prime Music — UX case study

UX case study by **Suvarna Myneni**, Senior Product / UX Designer. I led the design of Prime Music for the Amazon Digital Music Store across Kindle Fire, Fire Phone, iOS, Android, desktop, and web (September 2020 – May 2021).

**Live site:** https://suvarnamyneni-project.github.io/amazon-prime-music-case-study/

> In compliance with my non-disclosure agreement, I have intentionally omitted and obscured any confidential information in this project.

## Summary

Prime Music gave Prime members a curated catalogue of one million songs to stream and download at no extra cost. To launch fast, it was built inside the existing Digital Music Store and Music Library ("acquire, play, manage"). That decision shaped the experience: customers were confused about ownership, couldn't find Prime Music in a store, and saw buy buttons everywhere. Research, prototyping, and last-minute pivots got the product to launch. A post-launch diary study and journey map led to a Prime-only area that was fully separate from the store.

## Page sections

1. **Design Problem:** the shift to streaming, the good/fast/cheap architecture decision, and the waterfall process.
2. **Stakeholders:** PMs, the lead and platform designers, engineering, QA, and leadership; managing the "swinging pendulum" of feedback.
3. **End-users:** Prime members, non-Prime music buyers, and lean-back vs. lean-forward listeners.
4. **Primary Research:** a card sort (8 participants), a large-scale playlist survey, a button test (16 participants), an extended beta and guerrilla testing, and a post-launch diary study with usability testing.
5. **Secondary Research:** customer and market insights (for example, customers listen to 19% of their library, and collectors stream 4× as much), plus methods from *Metaskills* and *Gamestorming*.
6. **Competitive Analysis:** the streaming landscape and the team's "not a me-too" positioning.
7. **User Personas:** segments and goals from the source. There are no formal personas.
8. **Design Decisions:** design principles; playlists plus genres; the bargain bin; a single mood and genre filter list; the store SWOT; the $0.00 vs. +Add button; and the navigation and playback pivots.
9. **Prototype:** sketches, high-fidelity comps, sliced prototypes, and the CX spec journeys.
10. **UI Kit:** the Visual Design Spec and keylines, the type scale, grids, touch states, buttons, and motion timing.
11. **Final Designs:** Kindle Fire and Android screens.
12. **Outcome:** the launch, the post-launch redesign, customer quotes, and what I learned.
13. **Metrics:** +71% median active customers, +50% active days, +36% listening hours, and +31% new-customer retention.
